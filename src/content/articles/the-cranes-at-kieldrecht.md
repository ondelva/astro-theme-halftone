From the landward side the terminal announces itself before the water does. The ground here is flat enough that nothing interrupts the view, and the first thing to rise out of it is a line of ship-to-shore gantries, legs apart, booms up, standing in the same posture at the same interval for as far as the bank runs.

They are working cranes, not a monument, and they are arranged the way work arranges things: by the length of a ship. A container vessel comes alongside and the row is long enough that several gantries reach it at once, each one taking boxes off a different bay. The spacing in the photograph is not composition. It is the distance between hatches.

## What the ground was

Kieldrecht sits in Beveren, on the left bank, which is the newer half of the Port of Antwerp. The right bank is the old port, grown out from the city itself over centuries. The left bank is polder — land held below the level of the river and kept dry, farmed for generations, and then, in stages, dug into docks.

That history is visible in the foreground of the picture and nowhere else in it. The rough grass in front of the cranes is not landscaping. It is what the ground does when it is no longer farmed and not yet paved.

## The hour

The photograph was made in February, in the last of the light, which is the only hour when a terminal like this photographs as a shape rather than as a list of equipment. Under working light the frame would fill with detail: reach stackers, chassis, stacks sorted by line and by destination, the yellow and blue of one operator against the red of another.

At dusk the detail drops out and the row reads as a single object with a repeating element, which is closer to what it is. A container terminal is not a collection of machines that happen to stand together. It is one machine, sized to a ship, and the cranes are the part of it that shows above the horizon.

Nothing in the frame is moving. That is also true to the subject: a terminal spends a good part of its day waiting for the next vessel, and the cranes park in the same attitude, booms raised, while it waits.
