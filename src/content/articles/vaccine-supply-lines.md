The refrigerated van that supplies vaccines to nine clinics along the Verrow Plain leaves the Camborne Public Health Department's central depot at five each morning, running a route timed to keep doses within the two-to-eight-degree range required before the day's heat sets in.

This summer has tested that timing more than usual. Regional temperatures have run several degrees above average since June, and the depot's route coordinator, Nadia Ferro, says the margin between departure and the last clinic on the route has grown tighter.

"We used to have buffer built into the schedule," she said. "Now if the van sits at a checkpoint for twenty minutes, that's twenty minutes we didn't budget for."

The van carries a passive cooling system rather than active refrigeration, relying on ice packs rated for a twelve-hour window. On the route's longest day, covering the two farthest clinics near the Verrow Plain's southern edge, the van is on the road for just under ten hours.

Clinic staff at the last stop on the route, a two-room facility outside the town of Halden Crossing, say they have started checking delivery temperature logs more closely than the protocol technically requires.

"We haven't had a doses failure yet," said clinic nurse Ottavia Renke. "I don't want to be the clinic that finds out what happens when—"
